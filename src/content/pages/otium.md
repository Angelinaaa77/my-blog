*Otium* is the Roman word for time that is not owed to anyone — not leisure in
the sense of recovery, but the free attention out of which anything worth doing
eventually comes. Its opposite, *negotium*, literally means "not-otium":
business, the absence of the good thing. I find that framing more useful than
our own, which treats work as the default and free time as what is left over.

## Reading

I read slowly and finish maybe half of what I start, which I have stopped
feeling guilty about. Abandoning a book at page sixty is a judgement, and it is
usually correct. What I do finish, I tend to reread; the second pass is where
the argument actually lands, because the first is spent working out the shape
of the thing.

The standing interests: intellectual history, especially the moments when a
discipline changed its mind about what counted as an explanation. Long
biographies of people who were wrong in interesting ways. Anything written by
a practitioner rather than an observer.

## Music

I play badly and listen carefully, which is the correct ratio for an amateur.
Mostly early music and mid-century jazz — two traditions that share a quality
I find hard to name, where the structure is strict enough that the deviations
mean something.

I have a long-running and probably unfalsifiable theory that the music you
return to is the music that leaves room for you: pieces with enough space in
them that repeated listening keeps finding something. Density impresses on
first hearing and exhausts on the fourth.

## Walking, and looking at things

Most of what I have understood, I understood on a walk, some distance from the
desk where I had been failing to understand it. I do not think this is
mysterious. Walking occupies the part of the mind that generates interruptions,
and leaves the rest running.

The other half of this is looking at things properly — buildings, mostly, and
the parts of cities that were built for reasons nobody remembers. There is a
particular pleasure in working out why something is the shape it is.
